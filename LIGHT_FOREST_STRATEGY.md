# Light Forest Strategy

## Strategic lock

**Open protocol, closed consequences.**

The Light Forest is public, but it is not defenceless. Everyone can see the rules, offers, forecasts, receipts, and failures. Nobody receives access, authority, location, or capability merely by sending the correct bit.

## Universal codebook

- `1` = accept this exact micro-contract and advance one reversible step.
- `0` = reject or withdraw. No retaliation, penalty, or hostile classification.
- `NULL` = meaning, identity, authority, delivery, or outcome is unresolved. Freeze the state.

A `1` never means trusted, friendly, or come closer.

A `0` never means enemy.

`NULL` is the forest's primary defence against imagined meaning.

## Strategy hierarchy

1. Preserve continued existence.
2. Preserve agency and exit.
3. Learn about the counterpart.
4. Establish bounded cooperation.
5. Scale only after independent replication.

A lower objective can never override a higher one.

## Communication assumption

If only one bit can ever be exchanged, meaningful alignment is impossible unless the proposition and codebook were already shared.

If repeated pings are allowed, sequences of `0`, `1`, and `NULL` can establish syntax, continuity, reliability, and eventually limited cooperation. They still cannot prove moral character.

Good and bad are therefore poor permanent player classes. The safety of the next proposed action matters more than an assumed moral identity.

## Infinite-player strategy

Infinite interactions with a fixed nonzero catastrophic risk eventually produce ruin with probability approaching one.

Every contact therefore receives a shrinking risk budget:

```text
delta_n = Delta / (n * (n + 1))
```

where `Delta` is the maximum total existential-risk budget. Since the series sums to `Delta`, infinitely many encounters can be attempted while keeping theoretical cumulative exposure bounded.

The operational rule is:

> Unbounded discovery, bounded blast radius.

No individual contact can reach the entire network. No successful relationship becomes a universal trust credential. No failure can propagate through every layer.

## Promotion ladder

An actor advances only one level per completed test.

| Level | Demonstrated state |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | Signal detected |
| 1 | Shared syntax |
| 2 | Shared meaning for one proposition |
| 3 | Procedural reliability |
| 4 | Observable restraint |
| 5 | Bounded cooperation |
| 6 | Replicated cooperation across independent contexts |
| 7 | Federated relationship with local exit |

Promotion is based on receipts and outcomes, not eloquence or repeated `1` signals.

Trust decays over time, distance, regime change, model change, and unexplained interruption.

## TERRA tactics

TERRA is the protected root, not the experimental surface.

- Receive first.
- Do not connect received data to critical infrastructure.
- Treat executable payloads as hostile until isolated and understood.
- Transmit through remote, disposable relays rather than directly from essential systems.
- Separate public communication from coordinates, defences, biology, infrastructure, and capabilities.
- Require a diverse quorum before any consequential outbound move.
- Give no individual, government, company, religion, or model automatic authority to speak for Earth.
- Publish the codebook, forecasts, decisions, minority objections, and receipts.
- Run every new interaction inside a compartment with a predetermined maximum loss.
- Never allow urgency claims to bypass the board.

TERRA can be friendly in language while remaining extremely conservative in consequences.

## Milky Way tactics

The galaxy operates as a federation of autonomous firebreaks.

- Establish contact corridors through isolated relays.
- Keep each star system locally sovereign.
- Attach trust to specific receipt chains, not names or species.
- Prevent Sybil attacks by ensuring that creating more identities does not create more authority.
- Rate-limit signals, energy transfer, replication, and physical approach.
- Use delayed quorums because light-speed latency makes central command unreliable.
- Make every contract expire unless renewed.
- Let systems leave without being classified as defectors.
- Never propagate executable technology automatically between systems.
- Require independent replication before a local result becomes galactic policy.

A compromised node must remain a local tragedy, not a galactic contagion.

## Beyond the Milky Way

At intergalactic scale, identity continuity becomes highly uncertain.

A civilisation may disappear before its reply arrives. Its government, biology, machines, motives, and language may all change during one exchange.

- Every message must be self-contained and epoch-stamped.
- Silence resolves to `NULL`, never aggression.
- Old authority expires.
- Replies bind only the responding node and declared horizon.
- No retaliation is triggered by missing or malformed communication.
- Reputation travels more slowly than claims.
- Physical approach requires far stronger evidence than information exchange.
- Shared infrastructure is avoided until many independent cycles succeed.
- No single intergalactic protocol becomes a universal failure domain.

## Adversarial tactics

| Threat | Response |
| --- | --- |
| Friendly mimicry | Require costly, observable restraint rather than friendly language |
| Signal replay | Use round identifiers, timing windows, and non-repeating challenges |
| Sybil swarm | Cap influence by exposure and causal independence, not identity count |
| Flooding | Apply rate limits; excess traffic becomes `NULL` |
| Coercive urgency | Preserve the existing state until independently verified |
| Semantic poisoning | Freeze the codebook before every round |
| False authority | Require a traceable mandate and limited scope |
| Sleeper behaviour | Let trust decay; never grant permanent capability access |
| Collusion | Use independent observers and competing interpretations |
| Exploitation of silence | Treat silence as `NULL`, never consent |
| Correct-bit attack | Let a correct `1` advance only one sandboxed step |

## What the extinctions tell us

Countless destroyed civilisations are evidence of danger, but not evidence that the surviving players are evil.

Possible causes include conflict, accidents, environmental collapse, self-destruction, predators, failed coordination, or unknown natural processes.

The evidence should raise the prior probability of catastrophic interaction and systemic fragility. It should not classify an unknown responder as hostile without evidence.

## Win condition

The objective is not to contact everyone or identify the good civilisation.

The objective is:

> Maximise useful, voluntary cooperation while keeping cumulative existential exposure bounded and preserving every player's ability to stop, contest, correct, or leave.

The Light Forest remains light because its rules and evidence are visible.

It survives because its consequences remain local until cooperation earns the right to scale.
